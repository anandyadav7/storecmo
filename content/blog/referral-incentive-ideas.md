Referral incentive ideas usually get ranked by how generous they feel to a customer. A better ranking starts from what a referral actually leaves you: the profit left on a referred order, after landed cost, shipping and payment fees. The reward you can afford is whatever fits inside that profit with room to spare, and the reward type you choose decides whether it costs you full face value or a fraction of it.

The short answer: fixed store credit works when customers reorder often, a fixed cash or gift card amount works when they buy once every few years, and a percentage off only works when the catalog is cheap or the discount is capped. Free product, tiered and charity rewards are narrower cases.

## How do you price a referral incentive against your margin?

Start with gross profit per order, not [average order value](/tools/average-order-value-calculator). Talkable's [referral reward calculator](https://www.talkable.com/tools/referral-reward-calculator) frames the number that matters as cost per referred customer: the advocate's reward plus the friend's reward, counted the way each type is actually paid.

Take an illustrative store with an average order value of $60 and a 50% gross margin, which leaves $30 of gross profit on a referred order. A "give $15, get $15" cash offer costs $30 per referred customer, the entire gross profit, before shipping, pick and pack or payment fees even enter the picture. Break-even order value is the reward cost divided by the margin: above it each referral contributes profit, and below it each referral loses money. This store's $30 reward cost puts break-even at $60, exactly its average order value, so the referral covers its own reward with nothing left over.

Now offer $8 of store credit on each side instead of cash. Talkable notes that store credit only costs what customers redeem, so its cost is face value multiplied by your redemption rate. Assume 60% of credit gets redeemed (an illustrative figure; use your own once you have it): each side costs $4.80 and the pair costs $9.60. That drops break-even order value to $19.20, and roughly $20 of the original $30 of profit survives.

Same perceived generosity, a very different outcome. Talkable's own guardrail is to keep total reward cost under half of gross profit per order, leaving room for everything else the channel has to pay for.

## What does each referral reward actually cost per referral?

Here is how each one lands on your P&L.

| Reward type | What it costs you | Scales with order size? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percentage off | Order value multiplied by the percentage, paid in full at checkout | Yes |
| Fixed discount or credit at checkout | Face value, in full | No |
| Cash or gift card | Face value, in full, and the money leaves the business | No |
| Store credit or loyalty points | Face value multiplied by your redemption rate, and what is redeemed is spent back with you | No |
| Tiered rewards | Averaged across the tiers people actually reach | Depends on the tiers |

A percentage reward on a $300 order costs far more than the same percentage on a $40 order. Talkable's example is a 15% reward costing $45 on a $300 order, and it suggests watching percentage rewards closely on premium stores and capping the maximum discount.

What programs actually choose leans toward cash equivalents. [Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/referral/program-ideas/)'s October 2023 data on more than 500 programs found 61% gave referrers gift cards and 30% gave cash, while friends most often got a gift card (56%) or a discount (37%).

## Which referral reward fits your store profile?

Match the reward to how often customers buy, then check the result against the arithmetic above. The same margin question runs through [lead magnets for ecommerce](/blog/lead-magnets-for-ecommerce), which weighs what a discount-based offer costs in margin against an offer that costs none.

The split below is ours, built on Talkable's costing and Referral Rock's reward ideas:

- **Frequent, low-margin purchases:** store credit or points, since redemption keeps the real cost below face value and pulls a second order.
- **High-AOV, infrequent purchases:** a fixed cash amount or gift card, which Talkable says "reads as more premium and is easier to budget".
- **Healthy-margin, cheap catalog:** a capped percentage off.
- **Customers who would rather give than get:** a donation in the referrer's name, which Referral Rock suggests offering as one choice among rewards.

Fund the friend's side in every case, not only the referrer's.

## Should the friend get a reward too?

Yes, and fund it before adding to the referrer's own reward if money is tight. In Talkable's terms, a double-sided or "give-and-get" offer pays both the referrer and the new customer, while a single-sided offer pays only the referrer. Talkable says the give-and-get model "removes the awkwardness of asking a friend to buy with nothing in it for them", and in its 2025 BFCM campaign analysis, brands that removed the friend offer saw median conversion fall 13.86 percentage points.

Talkable also warns that a two-sided reward roughly doubles cost per referral, so recheck the combined total against the profit ceiling above rather than against one side alone. If that pushes the combined cost past half of gross profit, cut the per-side amount rather than dropping the friend's offer.

## When do tiered or milestone rewards pay off?

Tiered rewards (a bigger reward once someone has referred three, five or ten customers) pay off when a small group of customers refers repeatedly and the rest refer once or not at all. They protect margin on one-time sharers and spend more on the people actually bringing in revenue.

Cost tiers as a blended average across what people actually reach, not at the top tier. Suppose 90% of referrers earn a $10 first-tier reward and 10% reach a $25 tier: your planning number is roughly $11.50 per referral. That split is illustrative rather than observed, and your real exposure is whatever your single best advocate can earn in a month.

Keep the friend's offer the same at every tier, as Referral Rock recommends, so each friend still has a reason to say yes.

For a two-person team, add tiers after the flat program has run long enough to show whether repeat referrers exist at all. Built on day one, tiers are complexity you pay to administer for a behavior you have not seen yet.

## Does free shipping work as a referral reward?

Free shipping works as a referral reward where shipping cost is the visible barrier to a first order and you know what a parcel actually costs you to send.

On Shopify, free shipping is one of the [discount code types](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts) you can create, alongside a monetary value, a percentage and a buy X get Y code, entered by the customer at checkout. A Shopify discount applies to the order subtotal before tax, with tax calculated afterward, which matters when you are modeling the cost of a percentage reward against the free-shipping alternative.

[Free shipping vs flat rate](/blog/free-shipping-vs-flat-rate) covers how to set those rates and price the real cost behind them, and the [free shipping threshold calculator](/tools/free-shipping-threshold-calculator) sets the order value at which absorbing shipping stops costing you more than the referral is worth.

## How do you change a reward without guessing?

If you have the referral volume to test, run both rewards at the same time, not one after the other. Divide total reward payout by paying customers acquired, then compare that against gross profit per order. Referral platform [ReferralFlo](https://referralflo.ai/resources/blog/ab-test-referral-rewards-without-losing-margin) walks through why a reward that converts fewer referrals at a lower cost per acquisition can still beat one with a higher conversion rate.

For a lean store, the honest constraint is traffic. ReferralFlo notes that detecting a small lift in conversion rate needs a much larger sample, and most small programs will never get one. Our recommendation: if your referral volume is in the low dozens per month, do not run an A/B test. Change one thing, leave it for a full quarter, and judge it on gross profit after rewards rather than on referral count.

## Where does margin-first reward picking fail?

The arithmetic is only as good as its inputs, and four things break it.

**The redemption rate is an assumption, not a fact.** Store credit looks cheapest because you multiply it by a redemption rate; guess that rate and you guess your cost. Track what is actually redeemed over a quarter and recost the program from there.

**First-order math can be the wrong frame.** ReferralFlo points out that a referred customer often keeps buying, so a reward that loses money on the first order can still be the right call if lifetime value closes the gap on a later one. Check the second and third orders from referred customers before killing an offer that failed on order one.

**Codes leak.** A referral code is just a string typed at checkout, so once it is posted on a deal site it can be used by people it was never meant for. Talkable lists self-referrals, duplicate accounts and reward stacking as the ways payout quietly inflates, and says reward caps, eligibility rules and fraud checks keep the modeled cost honest.

**A program nobody sees costs nothing and earns nothing.** [LaunchMyStore](https://launchmystore.io/blog/ecommerce-referral-programs-brand-advocates), citing Extole, reports that poor promotion, not the reward itself, is the top reason referral programs fail. The ask has to live somewhere customers already are: the order confirmation, the post-delivery email, the account page. If you already send a post-purchase sequence, the [review request email template](/blog/review-request-email-template) covers when to send that message after delivery.

One more cost to budget: the app itself. [Smile.io's comparison of Shopify referral apps](https://smile.io/learn/best-referral-program-apps-for-shopify) splits pricing into two models: flat pricing by monthly order volume, or a monthly fee plus a success fee on referral sales (ReferralCandy's entry plan, for example, is $9 a month plus a 10.5% success fee). A success fee grows with every referral sale, so on a thin margin it becomes a second reward you did not plan for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a referral incentive?

A referral incentive is the reward a store pays when an existing customer brings in a new one. What actually decides its cost is whether the reward is paid in full or only when it is redeemed. Most ecommerce programs reward both sides of that introduction, so budget for the referrer's reward and the friend's reward together rather than just one.

### How much should a referral reward be?

Talkable's guidance is to size the combined reward, referrer plus friend, against gross profit on a referred order, never above it. Its practical guardrail is to keep that total under half of gross profit per order. Then check it against the break-even order value: if the reward pushes break-even above your actual average order value, the program loses money on every referral at that size.

### Should you offer store credit or cash?

Offer store credit if customers reorder within a few months, and cash or a gift card if they do not. Credit costs you face value multiplied by your redemption rate and gets spent back with you; cash costs full face value and leaves the business entirely. For a mattress or a sofa, credit toward a purchase years away is not a meaningful reward.

### Is a percentage discount a bad referral reward?

Not necessarily, but its cost moves with the basket size. A 20% reward costs $8 on a $40 order and $80 on a $400 one, so cap the maximum dollar discount on a premium catalog, or switch to a fixed amount. Fixed amounts also read as more concrete: [Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/do-referral-programs-work/), a loyalty and reviews vendor, notes that a "$20 credit" usually feels more significant than "20% off".
